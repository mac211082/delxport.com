Rice Bags for Export: PP Woven, BOPP, Jute, Non-Woven or Retail Pouch

Compare PP woven, BOPP laminated, jute, non-woven and retail pouch bags for export rice: strength, print, moisture, cost drivers and when to use each.

Rice15 Jul 20255 min readDELxPort

This guide compares the main bag types used for exporting rice from India, so you can pick the right one for your pack size, market and budget, and write a packing spec your supplier can quote against.

Five common options

Bag type Typical pack sizes Strength Print quality Relative cost per bag Best use
PP woven (plain or flexo printed) 10–50 kg High Basic, 1–3 colours Low Bulk, food service, wholesale
BOPP laminated PP woven 5–25 kg High Photographic, multi-colour Medium Branded retail and wholesale packs
Jute (hessian) 1–50 kg High, breathable Limited; printed or labelled Medium to high Premium and traditional packs, gifting
Non-woven PP 1–10 kg Moderate Screen or flexo; laminated versions available Low to medium Retail carry bags, promotional packs
Retail pouches (laminated film) 0.5–5 kg Moderate; depends on film Excellent Medium per kg, high setup Supermarket shelves, private label

The cost column is a rough ranking only. Actual cost depends on the drivers listed further down.

PP woven bags

Plain woven polypropylene is the default bag for bulk rice. Flat tapes of PP are woven into fabric on circular or flat looms, then cut and sewn into bags.

  • Strength: high tear and burst resistance for its weight. Handles stacking and manual loading well.
  • Breathability: the weave lets air pass, which helps moisture escape and fumigant reach the rice.
  • Print: usually flexographic, a few colours. Good enough for brand name, variety and net weight.
  • Options: inner PE liner for moisture protection, UV stabiliser if bags will be stored outdoors, anti-slip coating for stable stacks.

Use it for: 25 kg and 50 kg sacks for wholesalers, food service and re-packers, where the bag is not on a retail shelf.

BOPP laminated woven bags

These start as PP woven fabric. A biaxially oriented polypropylene (BOPP) film is reverse printed, usually by rotogravure, and laminated onto the fabric.

  • Print: the print sits under the film, so it doesn't rub off. Multi-colour and photographic artwork is possible.
  • Strength: same base fabric as PP woven, with a smoother surface that resists scuffing.
  • Moisture: the film slows moisture and air exchange. That protects against humidity but can trap moisture in rice packed warm. Mills often add micro-perforations to let air out during filling and stacking.
  • Options: handles, gussets, matte or gloss finish, easy-open stitching, clear windows.

Use it for: branded 5–25 kg packs in Gulf, African and Asian retail and cash-and-carry, where the bag is the shelf display.

Jute bags

Jute is the traditional sack for grain. It is natural, biodegradable and breathable, and many buyers associate it with premium basmati.

  • Strength: strong and holds its shape when stacked.
  • Food safety: jute fibre is processed with batching oil. For food, specify bags made with food-grade, vegetable-based batching oil and low oil content, or use an inner food-grade liner. Indian jute bag standards for foodgrain set a maximum oil content of 3% on a dry basis.
  • Print: simple screen printing or sewn labels. Fine detail is hard to achieve.
  • Downsides: heavier than PP, so it adds to freight weight; can shed fibres; costs more per bag.

Use it for: premium retail and gifting packs, often 1–10 kg with a drawstring or inner pouch, and markets where customers prefer natural packaging.

Non-woven bags

Non-woven PP is made by bonding fibres into a fabric sheet without weaving. It is the material of many reusable shopping bags.

  • Strength: adequate for 1–10 kg with sewn handles. Not suited to heavy sacks or rough handling.
  • Print: screen or flexo on plain fabric. Laminated non-woven allows fuller artwork.
  • Customer value: the bag can be reused as a shopping bag after the rice is used, which some retailers use as a selling point.
  • Moisture: fabric is porous; add an inner liner if the route is humid.

Use it for: retail and promotional packs, festive offers, hampers.

Retail pouches

For supermarket shelves, rice is packed in multi-layer laminated film pouches. Common formats include pillow packs, stand-up zip pouches and vacuum-packed bricks.

  • Structure: typical laminates combine PET for print and stiffness with PE for sealing. Metallised layers add a barrier. Nylon is added for vacuum packs to resist puncture.
  • Print: rotogravure, high quality, many colours.
  • Protection: the best moisture and odour barrier of the options here. Vacuum packs also reduce insect risk and keep the pack compact.
  • Outer packing: pouches need master cartons or outer bale bags for shipping. That adds cost and reduces net rice per container compared with bulk sacks.

Use it for: private-label supermarket packs, 0.5–5 kg, where shelf appearance and barcode compliance matter.

What drives bag cost

  • Fabric weight (GSM) and tape strength. Heavier fabric costs more and adds freight weight. Match GSM to fill weight and handling.
  • Lamination and film. BOPP lamination, metallised layers and nylon all add cost per bag.
  • Number of colours and print method. Rotogravure needs an engraved cylinder for each colour. Cylinders are a one-time cost per design, so small runs of a new design cost more per bag.
  • Minimum order quantities. Printed bags and pouches have MOQs. Changing artwork often raises your average cost.
  • Features. Handles, zips, gussets, windows, liners, valves and easy-open seams each add cost.
  • Freight effect. Bag weight counts against container payload. Cartons and pallets for retail packs take space and weight that bulk sacks don't.

Practical points for export

  • Labelling. Check destination rules for language, importer details, net weight, lot number, and packing and best-before dates. Many retailers specify a barcode format too.
  • Fumigation. Sea cargo is fumigated. Breathable bags help fumigant reach the rice. With laminated bags and pouches, agree how fumigation will be done.
  • Moisture at packing. Rice packed warm in sealed or laminated bags can sweat. Ask for packing after the rice has cooled.
  • Food contact. Ask the bag maker for a food-contact compliance declaration suited to your market.
  • Spare bags. Ask for a small percentage of empty spare bags to replace any damaged in transit.

Before you order

  • Pack size and fill weight
  • Bag type, fabric GSM and dimensions
  • Print colours and artwork approval process
  • Liner, handles, gussets or zip if needed
  • Label content for your destination
  • Master cartons or bale bags for retail packs
  • Spare empty bags per container
